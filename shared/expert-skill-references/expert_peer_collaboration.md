# Authorized expert-peer collaboration

Read only when the user explicitly appoints an expert partner to answer, challenge or help explore the current research task. Ordinary human collaboration remains the default. This reference supplies no permission to create agents/chats, send messages, change models, spend an additional budget, run experiments or mutate another project; use only the separately authorized channel and scope.

## Roles and authority

Recover the user's assignment: who proposes and asks, who answers as an expert partner, and who reviews. A literature lookup or end-of-run review is not a substitute when the user asked for iterative question/answer collaboration. Do not require the human to answer routine technical questions that the appointed expert can investigate.

The expert may challenge source interpretation, causal assumptions, alternatives and feasibility. Personal preferences, tolerable quality loss, resource access, deadlines and authority to execute remain the human user's unless explicitly supplied. Mark missing user-owned facts as unknown; do not let a plausible peer answer manufacture them.

## An evidence-changing exchange

Bring a concrete candidate and decisive evidence, ask about the assumption or failure case that could change it, and have the expert give a reasoned answer or counterexample. The proposing side then verifies the relevant source and revises, rejects or retains the candidate with reasons. Continue where another consequential uncertainty remains, without a fixed number of turns or a theatrical role-play transcript.

When the user requested both answers and adversarial review, include constructive work too: explain a better mapping, adaptation or test when possible, not just repeated vetoes. Cross-domain proposals still need the source problem, working mechanism, natural target condition, broken assumptions, changed design and cost.

Keep a compact working record of question, sourced answer, disagreement and its effect on the candidate. Repeated agreement, a longer bibliography and the number of topics do not establish maturity. Search-round consolidation should expose the decisive unresolved issue; it must not silently stop an authorized continuing task or continue after an explicit pause.

## Return to the human

Report what the peer exchange changed, surviving evidence and counterevidence, and decisions still owned by the user. “Both peers currently favor testing this” is a provisional research judgment, not human confirmation, proof of novelty, performance evidence or a venue acceptance prediction. Do not declare that the human has understood a concept merely because the peer can explain it.
