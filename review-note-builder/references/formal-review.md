# Formal review guide

The formal reviewer report is a second artifact. Write it for authors and the venue, using the requested form if supplied. Without a venue form, use these sections:

- **Summary and High Level Discussion:** Briefly explain the paper's problem, method, evidence, recommendation, and decisive reason for that recommendation.
- **Strengths:** Give specific, source-supported contributions and results. A well-executed integration can be a strength even when its components are established.
- **Weaknesses:** Prioritize the few concerns that actually lower confidence or recommendation. For each, state the paper location, consequence, and smallest resolution. Do not turn every five-question observation into a weakness.
- **Comments for Rebuttal:** Ask only answerable questions whose responses could materially change the assessment.
- **Detailed Comments for Authors:** Give smaller technical, claim-boundary, writing, reference, and presentation fixes with locations. Keep them separate from major weaknesses.
- **Overall Recommendation:** Reconcile the strengths and weaknesses, provide the recommendation and confidence, and state what a rebuttal could change.

Include a reproducibility section when the evidence makes it material. Include scores, venue-specific questions, and confidential committee comments only when the user or venue requires them. Do not copy the private five-question analysis wholesale or include a user-note correction ledger in a report intended for authors.

Before delivering, compare the analysis note and formal report. Each formal weakness must be traceable to a tested five-question finding. The severity, cited evidence, interpretation, requested fix, and final recommendation must match. The formal report may be shorter and more selective; it must not silently change a concern from “the paper does not explain this” to “the method is wrong,” or downgrade a confirmed issue without explaining why. Keep the user's latest teacher-agreed judgment visible in both artifacts unless primary evidence forces an explicitly explained revision.
