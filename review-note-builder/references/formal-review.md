# Formal review guide

Use this guide only for Stage 2, after the user has presented and supplies the PPT, discussion/teacher conclusions, and edited Stage 1 analysis note. The formal reviewer report is the Stage 2 deliverable; the presentation-ready analysis note is already a separate earlier artifact. Write for authors and the venue, using the requested form if supplied. Without a venue form, use these sections:

- **Summary and High Level Discussion:** First give a neutral, source-faithful account of the paper's problem, method, and evidence; then state the reviewer's assessment and decisive reason for the recommendation. Paraphrase the abstract and relevant method/results passages precisely rather than replacing them with a plausible but different mechanism. The user's revised analysis and post-discussion conclusion set the intended emphasis, but do not override factual evidence in the manuscript.
- **Strengths:** Give specific, source-supported contributions and results. A well-executed integration can be a strength even when its components are established.
- **Weaknesses:** Prioritize the few concerns that actually lower confidence or recommendation. For each, state the paper location, consequence, and smallest resolution. Do not turn every five-question observation into a weakness.
- **Comments for Rebuttal:** Ask only answerable questions whose responses could materially change the assessment.
- **Detailed Comments for Authors:** Give smaller technical, claim-boundary, writing, reference, and presentation fixes with locations. Keep them separate from major weaknesses.
- **Overall Recommendation:** Reconcile the strengths and weaknesses, provide the recommendation and confidence, and state what a rebuttal could change.

Include a reproducibility section when the evidence makes it material. Include scores, venue-specific questions, and confidential committee comments only when the user or venue requires them; match the venue's exact scale and wording. Do not copy the private analysis wholesale or include a user-note correction ledger in a report intended for authors.

Before delivering, compare the analysis note and formal report. Each formal weakness must be traceable to a tested five-question finding. The severity, cited evidence, interpretation, requested fix, and final recommendation must match. The formal report may be shorter and more selective; it must not silently change a concern from “the paper does not explain this” to “the method is wrong,” or downgrade a confirmed issue without explaining why. Keep the user's latest teacher-agreed judgment visible in both artifacts unless primary evidence forces an explicitly explained revision.

Before delivery, reconcile the final report with the user's edited analysis, PPT, and latest discussion conclusion. Preserve the presentation's accepted storyline and explicitly incorporate new corrections where supported. Every substantive summary, strength, and weakness must trace to the paper with accurate scope, causal direction, comparison, terminology, and evidence qualifier. Do not turn an inference such as “the method may help because…” into “the authors show…”. Do not treat a gap in a draft note as a paper flaw or retain a pre-discussion concern the user later corrected without rechecking it. Keep major acceptance-affecting concerns separate from minor wording and cross-reference fixes. Write professionally and concretely, without inflated praise, unsupported criticism, or generic review language that could fit any paper.
